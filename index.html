About

I am a geologist and mineralogist working on natural and synthetic minerals and inorganic materials. My research rests on combining electron microscopy (SEM and HRTEM) with synchrotron radiation X-ray absorption spectroscopy (XANES and EXAFS), an approach few geoscientists apply together. It lets me follow trace metals to the micro- and nanometre scale where they actually reside and determine their chemical state there.

I studied Geology & Geoenvironment at the National and Kapodistrian University of Athens (NKUA), where I also completed my PhD on the mineralogy and geochemistry of the Parnassos-Ghiona bauxites and their red mud residues. As a Marie Curie postdoctoral fellow (H.C. Ørsted COFUND) at the Center for Electron Nanoscopy of the Technical University of Denmark, I investigated rare earth elements and actinides such as thorium in ores and metallurgical materials. I then worked at the Department of Materials Engineering, KU Leuven, and at NCSR “Demokritos” before joining the Technical University of Crete in 2020.

I am an advanced user of the SUL-X beamline at the KIT Light Source (Karlsruhe) and a user of the DIFFABS beamline at Synchrotron SOLEIL. I review for international journals, act as an expert evaluator for Horizon Europe, and am a member of the Geochemical Society. As a former Marie Skłodowska-Curie fellow, I am an active member of the Marie Curie Alumni Association (MCAA), in both its Denmark and Greece Chapters.

Signature expertise

Over nearly two decades I have built hands-on experience in a state-of-the-art combination of two techniques that is rarely mastered by the same geoscientist: electron microscopy and synchrotron X-ray absorption spectroscopy. Used together on the same samples, they answer questions neither can answer alone.

Electron microscopy

SEM & HRTEM

Shows where an element is and what hosts it: morphology, crystal structure and chemistry from the microscale down to the atomic world (SEM-EDS/WDS, TEM, SAED, EELS).

+
Synchrotron radiation

XANES & EXAFS

Shows what form the element takes: oxidation state, coordination and local bonding environment, even at trace concentrations (μ-XRF, μ-XANES/EXAFS).

Research

Among other studies of (geo)-/(bio)-materials, my research helps the mining and metallurgical industry recover valuable metals from ores and residues with lower risk for nearby communities, towards near-zero-waste processing, with the remains converted into building and engineered materials.

Primary ore deposits

Mineralogy and geochemistry of ores and potential new mineral resources, with a focus on how base, critical and noble metals are hosted.

  • Karst bauxites (Greece, Balkans, Hungary)
  • Ni-Fe laterites, chromites and Mn ores (Greece)
  • “Invisible” gold in mixed sulfides (Kassandra mines)
  • REE ores from the Nordic region and Greenland
  • REY-rich deep-sea muds, NW Pacific
  • Coastal placer sands, zeolite and clay deposits

Mineral waste streams

Characterization and valorisation of residues to recover metals and turn the remainder into building materials.

  • Bauxite residue (red mud): REE, Th, Fe and Al recovery
  • Metallurgical slags and secondary lead slag
  • Hospital medical waste incinerator ash
  • Inorganic polymers and alkali-activated materials
  • H₂ and microwave routes to low-carbon ironmaking

Isotopes, heritage & extraterrestrial materials

Stable isotopes (δ¹⁸O, δ¹³C, δ¹⁵N, δ³⁴S) as proxies for environment, diet and provenance, plus the mineralogy of meteorites.

  • Carbonates, speleothems and paleoclimate
  • Historic mortars and building materials
  • Bioapatite, ancient diet and mobility
  • Food authenticity (olive oil, wine, bread)
  • Shocked ordinary chondrites

Calcified aortic valves (biomineralization)

Structural and elemental analysis of calcified aortic valves from a rabbit in vivo model and human samples under diabetic and non-diabetic conditions. A collaboration between TUC, NKUA, University of Bern and the Academy of Athens.

  • A combination of nano-XRF elemental mapping along with synchrotron radiation (SR) techniques on calcified aortic valves (CAVS) from rabbit specimens and human samples under diabetic (DM) and non-diabetic (non-DM) conditions
  • SR micro-XRF/-XRD/-XAFS measurements
  • Deepening our knowledge of the chemical and mineralogical composition of aortic valve calcification, potentially enhancing our mechanistic understanding of the disease process and identifying element-specific targets that could inform future therapeutic strategies for slowing or preventing disease progression
